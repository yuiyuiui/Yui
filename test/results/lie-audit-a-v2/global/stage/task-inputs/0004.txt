## Definitions needed to read the exercise
The original representation is in characteristic zero: take F=C and
L=sl(2,C), with Chevalley generators x,y,h satisfying
[h,x]=2x, [h,y]=-2y, [x,y]=h. V(lambda) is its finite-dimensional
irreducible highest-weight module of highest weight the nonnegative integer
lambda. A highest-weight vector v_plus is nonzero, x v_plus=0 and
h v_plus=lambda v_plus. Equivalently this is the standard simple
(lambda+1)-dimensional characteristic-zero module; its integral form, not
the already-reduced characteristic-p irreducibility, is used in the construction.

The integral universal enveloping algebra U_Z is the Z-subalgebra of U(L)
generated by the divided powers x^r/r! and y^r/r! for all integers r>=0.
A lattice M in V(lambda) is a free abelian subgroup with a Z-basis that is
also a C-basis of V(lambda). It is admissible when U_Z M is contained in M
(section 27.1, printed page 159). M is an arbitrary such admissible lattice;
its choice must not silently be restricted to the minimal lattice.

K is any field of positive characteristic p (section 27.4), with p prime.
V(K) means M tensor_Z K. The Chevalley Z-form L(Z) is the Z-span of x,y,h,
and L(K)=L(Z) tensor_Z K acts on V(K) by the induced integral actions.
This is the usual sl(2,K) Chevalley Lie algebra, including characteristic 2.
The construction can be defined directly by these operators also for the
trivial highest-weight module; the exercise explicitly includes lambda=0.

An L(K)-submodule is a K-linear subspace invariant under all the induced
operators x,y,h. Irreducible means the module is nonzero and every such
subspace is either zero or the whole module.

## Original target
For every prime p, every field K of characteristic p, every integer
lambda with 0<=lambda<p, and every admissible lattice M in V(lambda),
prove that V(K)=M tensor_Z K is irreducible as an L(K)-module.

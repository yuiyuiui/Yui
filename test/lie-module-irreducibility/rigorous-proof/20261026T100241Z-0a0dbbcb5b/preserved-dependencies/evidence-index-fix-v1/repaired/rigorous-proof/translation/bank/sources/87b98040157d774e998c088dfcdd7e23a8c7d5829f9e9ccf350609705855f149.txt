### Lemma 2.1: a uniform Fresnel--Abel bound

There is an absolute constant \(C_{\mathrm{Fr}}\) such that

\[
 \sup_{\substack{a>0\\c\in\mathbb R}}
 \left|\int_0^\infty e^{i(s-c)^2}e^{-as}\,ds\right|
 \le C_{\mathrm{Fr}}.
 \tag{2.1a}
\]

To prove this, first observe that the primitive of \(e^{ir^2}\) is bounded
uniformly on every interval.  On the part of an interval contained in
\([-1,1]\), this follows from its length.  On \([1,\infty)\), and similarly
on \((-\infty,-1]\), integration by parts using

\[
 e^{ir^2}=\frac1{2ir}\frac{d}{dr}e^{ir^2}
\]

shows that the integral over every subinterval is bounded by an absolute
constant: both endpoint terms are bounded and the remaining integrand is
\(O(r^{-2})\).  Translation by \(c\) therefore gives

\[
 \sup_{c\in\mathbb R}\sup_{S>0}
 \left|\int_0^S e^{i(s-c)^2}\,ds\right|\le C.
\]
